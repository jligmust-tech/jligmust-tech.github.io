Timeline review: perturbation screens and the virtual cell, 2001–2026

The stage-1 guide ranked perturbation models, listed benchmarks, and argued for evaluating embeddings without mistaking systematic variation for biology. It described the field as a set of competing representations. The historical record suggests a simpler engine: the field moves when a new perturbation modality becomes cheap, and the models follow a few years later.

That ordering matters, because it predicts where the next jump comes from. If models lead, the next advance is a better architecture. If assays lead, the next advance is a cheaper way to perturb and measure — and the current bottleneck is not representation quality but the number of held-out perturbations anyone has measured.

Scope note: companion review, not a systematic history. Dates mark first credible publication or deployment, not adoption. No screens were re-analyzed and no benchmark was run for this post. The chart in §2 is a schematic of modality availability, not a bibliometric count.
Central claim: every capability jump in perturbation biology came from a new assay — siRNA, pooled CRISPR, Perturb-seq, optical pooling — and every model class arrived after the data it needed already existed. The virtual cell is therefore less a modeling program than a data-coverage program, and its hard problem is generalization across perturbations, not across cell lines.

1. Why a timeline here

Perturbation data is unusual in one respect: the axis that matters is not time or tissue but which interventions are measurable at all. A model that predicts knockdowns is not a weaker version of a model that predicts knockouts; it is a model of a different intervention class, with different off-target structure and different noise. The history of the field is a history of expanding that intervention column, and each expansion invalidated a benchmark without invalidating the biology.

Two questions follow directly. When did each modality become available, and did the models that claim to generalize actually have the data to support the claim at that moment? The answer to the second question is uncomfortable often enough to be worth writing down.

3. The record

  • 2001–2012 · loss of function becomes cheap · readout: viability, bulk
  • 2001siRNA in mammalian cells — RNAi becomes a reagent rather than a phenomenon; genome-wide screens follow within two years.
  • 2003First genome-wide RNAi screens in fly and worm; the field learns that knockdown efficiency is itself a variable that must be measured.
  • 2006Connectivity Map — 164 compounds, a handful of cell lines, and the idea that a transcriptional signature is a perturbation fingerprint.
  • 2012Programmable CRISPR–Cas9 — the intervention becomes a sequence, which means it becomes a column in a table and a covariate in a model.
  • 2013–2018 · screens at scale, single-cell readout · readout: counts per cell
  • 2013–14Pooled genome-wide CRISPR screens in human cells; dropout and enrichment become the standard readout, and essentiality maps appear.
  • 2014CRISPRi and CRISPRa — repression and activation give the field dose-like control and a second direction of effect, which turns out to matter enormously for model validation.
  • 2016Perturb-seq — pooled perturbations read out by single-cell transcriptomics; the unit of observation becomes (cell, perturbation, transcriptome).
  • 2016Cell Painting — morphological profiling as a cheap, high-content readout that is not transcriptomic and therefore not redundant with it.
  • 2017L1000 / CMap 2.0 — a million-odd profiles at scale; the first resource where "perturbation space" is a real thing to interpolate in.
  • 2018scVI — probabilistic latent-variable modelling of single-cell counts; the embedding substrate that later perturbation models inherit.
  • 2019–2021 · spatial and optical pooling · readout: images plus sequence
  • 2019Optical pooled screens — perturbation identity read out in situ, so imaging and genotype share an experiment; spatial phenotypes become accessible to screens.
  • 2019–21Atlases and harmonized resources — cell atlases and perturbation compendia make cross-study comparison possible, at the cost of batch structure nobody fully models.
  • 2021–22Single-cell foundation models — scBERT, then Geneformer and scGPT; a shared representation is proposed as the substrate for perturbation prediction.
  • 2022–2024 · prediction benchmarks · readout: predicted post-perturbation state
  • 2022–23GEARS — graph-based prediction over gene and perturbation relations, evaluated on held-out perturbations; the evaluation split becomes the argument.
  • 2023Geneformer, scGPT — large-scale pretraining on unperturbed cells, then fine-tuning to perturbation tasks; the question of whether unperturbed pretraining teaches perturbation response is left open.
  • 2024scPerturb and harmonized benchmarks — the field gets a common dataset layer and a common complaint: results depend on the split.
  • 2024LLM-based annotation of cell identity — marker rules proposed by a model and falsified against expression; a cheap, high-throughput check on vocabulary.
  • 2025–2026 · virtual-cell programmes · readout: contested
  • 2025Virtual-cell challenges and consortia — benchmarked prediction of perturbation response across cell types, with leaderboards and, for once, explicit split definitions.
  • 2025–26State-space and multimodal cell models — perturbation, imaging, and expression pushed into one latent space; the claim under test is transfer across modalities rather than within one.
  • 2026This review — the field's bottleneck is stated as data coverage over perturbations, not representation capacity. That is a falsifiable claim, and §6 says how to test it.

4. Assays moved the field

Look at the lag between the bottom row and the rows above it. In-silico prediction begins around 2022, eleven years after the first pooled CRISPR screen and six after Perturb-seq. Every model class in the field is downstream of an assay that made its target variable measurable. There is no case in this record where a model created a new class of observable.

That asymmetry has three consequences.

  • Benchmarks are modality-specific, and so are the models. A model trained on CRISPR knockout screens has seen a particular off-target and efficiency distribution. Transferring it to CRISPRi is not a domain shift in the usual sense; the intervention's mechanism has changed. Papers that report one number across modalities are usually reporting an average of two different tasks.
  • New assays reset the credibility of old claims. When optical pooled screens arrived, every claim about "morphological response" made from bulk readouts became a claim about a proxy. The models did not get worse; the target moved.
  • The next jump is likely an assay, not an architecture. The obvious candidates are cheaper combinatorial perturbations (two genes at once), perturbation in primary and spatially resolved tissue, and time-resolved readouts. Each would invalidate the current benchmark suite the way Perturb-seq invalidated bulk screens.
Prediction, stated so it can fail: a perturbation model that leads with architecture and reports a single pooled benchmark will not survive the next modality change. A model that reports per-modality performance, per-gene-held-out performance, and its split definition will. The second kind is rarer and more useful, and it is a reporting choice, not a modeling one.

5. The validation problem is the whole problem

The guide's central methodological warning — do not mistake systematic variation for biology — has a precise historical form in this field: how you split determines what you have shown. Three splits are routinely conflated.

SplitWhat it testsWhat it flattersTypical result
Cell held outTransfer across cell contexts for known perturbationsBatch correction and donor structure; the perturbation identity is memorizedHigh scores, weak mechanism
Gene held outGeneralization to unseen regulatorsGene-network priors if the graph leaks; nearest-neighbour structure in expression spaceModerate scores, honest mechanism
Both held outThe virtual-cell claim properAlmost nothing — this is the hard testLow scores, real information

The record shows why this matters more here than elsewhere. Because each new modality arrives with a new set of measured perturbations, the number of distinct perturbations is always small relative to the number of cells, and the number of genes is always small relative to the number of possible interventions. A model can therefore look excellent on cells and poor on interventions, and the standard random split will report the first number. Gene-held-out is the only split that corresponds to the virtual-cell promise, because a virtual cell is exactly a claim about interventions nobody has run.

There is a second, subtler leak. Perturbation identity often correlates with expression state for trivial reasons: essential genes kill cells, and dead cells have a signature. A model that learns "this perturbation is lethal" scores well on viability-correlated tasks and learns nothing about regulation. The historical fix is not a better loss function; it is a control — non-targeting guides, safe-harbour controls, and matched viability strata reported alongside the headline number.

6. Where 2026 sits

A short, opinionated position table. Each row is a claim the field makes, and what would count as evidence for it.

ClaimStatusEvidence that would settle it
Perturbation response is predictable within a modalityPartly supported; effect sizes depend on the splitStandardized gene-held-out evaluation across ≥3 screens with per-gene reporting
Response transfers across modalitiesWeakly supported; mostly untestedSame gene, matched cell line, CRISPRi vs knockout vs ORF, predicted direction agreement
Response transfers across cell typesPartly supported for strong effectsHeld-out tissue with pre-registered effect-size floor
Pretraining on unperturbed cells helpsUnclear; confounded by capacity and data volumeCompute-matched control, same architecture, with and without pretraining
Embeddings capture mechanismNot supported by current evaluationsRecovery of known pathway structure under perturbation, not just cluster separation
Combinatorial perturbation is predictableOpen; data is the binding constraintHeld-out pairs, not held-out cells, with measured epistasis

The pattern across the rows is the same one the timeline shows: the field is data-limited in the specific sense that the interventions it wants to predict have not been measured. Better representations help at the margin. More measured perturbations help at the core, and they help every model class at once — which is why the resource papers in the list above matter more than their citation counts suggest.

Closing thoughts

The interesting thing about this timeline is how little of it is about models. The field's real progress is a sequence of instruments that made new columns of the perturbation table measurable, followed by models that learned whatever the new columns contained. The virtual-cell programme inherits that structure whether or not it admits it: the promise is about interventions nobody has run, and the only evidence that can support it comes from interventions somebody has.

So the useful question in 2026 is not "which representation is best." It is "which unmeasured perturbation would you most want to predict, and what is the cheapest experiment that would test your prediction?" A field that answers that question with experiments will accumulate a virtual cell. A field that answers it with benchmarks will accumulate leaderboards.
